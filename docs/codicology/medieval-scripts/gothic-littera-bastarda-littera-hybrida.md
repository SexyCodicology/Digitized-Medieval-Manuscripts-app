# Bastarda (Littera Bastarda, Littera Hybrida)

Littera Bastarda was a medieval script commonly used in Europe during the
fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. It combines features of Gothic and
[Humanistic styles](humanistic-script.md) and appears in legal documents,
religious texts, and other late-medieval manuscripts.

## Origin and Characteristics

Littera Bastarda, also known as Bastard Script or Bastarda, is thought to have
originated in the early fourteenth century, possibly in France or England. It
was a transitional script that combined Humanistic influences, rooted in
classical Roman writing, with the Gothic script prevalent in medieval Europe.

The Littera Bastarda was a cursive script with a number of unique features. It was characterized by its irregular, fluid strokes and its mix of Gothic and Humanistic letterforms. The script was also known for its use of ligatures — the joining of two or more letters into a single glyph — which helped save space and improve legibility, important for legal documents and other official records.

Like many medieval manuscripts, it also uses abbreviations. These save space and
speed writing, but can make the text difficult for modern readers to read.

## Usage and Importance

Littera Bastarda was widely used in late-medieval Europe for charters, deeds,
wills, religious texts, literary manuscripts such as works by Chaucer, and
personal correspondence.

*Example of Littera Bastarda in Harley, ms 4866, fs001r — [The British Library](../../libraries/the-british-library-digitised-manuscripts-49.md).*

Littera Bastarda is important because it forms a bridge between Gothic and
Humanistic styles. It reflects a period of cultural change as Renaissance ideas
influenced art, literature, science, and writing.

Its ligatures and abbreviations influenced early printing typefaces, and its
irregular strokes helped inspire italic typefaces.

## Decline and Legacy

The Littera Bastarda began to decline in popularity in the late 15th century, as the Humanistic script became more dominant in Europe. However, it continued to be used in certain regions, such as Spain and Portugal, well into the 16th century.

Despite its decline, Littera Bastarda has had a lasting legacy. Its blend of
Gothic and Humanistic letterforms has inspired modern typefaces, and its use of
ligatures and abbreviations continues to influence typography and graphic design.

## Legacy

Littera Bastarda remains important to the history of European writing,
typography, and graphic design. Calligraphers, typographers, and historians
continue to study its blend of Gothic and Humanistic elements. Surviving
documents and manuscripts provide valuable insight into medieval life and the
importance of preserving cultural heritage.
